Mixed Models

Using Margins.jl with MixedModels.jl

Overview

Margins.jl supports linear and generalized linear mixed models fit with MixedModels.jl through a package extension that loads when both packages are imported. Every analysis of the other model classes works on them: population_margins, profile_margins, scenarios, groups, contrasts and second differences.

A mixed model's predictions depend on the random effects as well as on β, so a marginal effect of a mixed model is not one quantity. The keyword random_effects says which one you want:

random_effectsthe random effects arethe estimates describe
:zero (default)set to 0a typical group: one whose random effects are 0
:conditionaleach row's predicted random effects b̂the groups in the data
:marginalaveraged over their estimated distributionthe population, averaged over groups
a NamedTuple, e.g. (village = :conditional, rater = :marginal)a mode per grouping factore.g. the villages in the data, with a new rater from the rater population

Under an identity link (an LMM, or a GLMM with an identity link) :marginal equals :zero, and average effects of variables without random slopes are the same in all three modes; the choice matters for predictions (:conditional vs the others) and, under a nonlinear link, for everything. Under a nonlinear link the effect at random effects 0 is not the population-averaged effect: the average of g⁻¹(η + z′b) over b is flatter than g⁻¹(η), so :zero overstates population-averaged effects on the probability scale.

Example

Ratings by 30 raters in 12 villages (crossed), a binary outcome, a random intercept and a random slope on w for raters, and a random intercept for villages:

using Random, DataFrames, CategoricalArrays, MixedModels, Margins
rng = Random.Xoshiro(2026)
nr, nv, per = 30, 12, 4
d = DataFrame(rater = repeat(string.("r", lpad.(1:nr, 2, '0')), inner = nv * per),
              village = repeat(repeat(string.("v", lpad.(1:nv, 2, '0')), inner = per), nr))
n = nrow(d)
d.x = randn(rng, n); d.w = randn(rng, n)
d.treat = categorical(rand(rng, ["control", "treated"], n))
a_r = randn(rng, nr); s_r = 0.5 .* randn(rng, nr); a_v = 0.8 .* randn(rng, nv)
ri = parse.(Int, chop.(d.rater; head = 1, tail = 0)); vi = parse.(Int, chop.(d.village; head = 1, tail = 0))
η = -0.3 .+ 0.8 .* d.x .+ 0.4 .* d.w .+ 0.6 .* (d.treat .== "treated") .+ a_r[ri] .+ s_r[ri] .* d.w .+ a_v[vi]
d.y = rand.(rng) .< 1 ./ (1 .+ exp.(-η))

m = fit(MixedModel, @formula(y ~ x + w + treat + (1 + w | rater) + (1 | village)), d, Bernoulli();
        progress = false)

random_effects = :conditional

Each row's predicted random effects (the conditional modes b̂) enter the linear predictor, so a row is predicted for its own rater and village. In a profile grid, set the grouping columns to choose the groups; re_terms conditions on some grouping factors and sets the others to 0:

julia> profile_margins(m, d, DataFrame(x = 0.0, w = 0.0, treat = "control", village = ["v01", "v02"]);
                       type = :predictions, random_effects = :conditional, re_terms = :village)

A level absent from the fit (a new village in a grid, or in new data) gets random effects 0 under re_new = :zero (the default); re_new = :error rejects it.

A grid cell may hold a categorical mixture, as means_grid fills one into every categorical column it is not given. Margins evaluates a mixture by setting the dummy columns to the shares (as Stata's atmeans does), and the random effects follow the same rule: a mixture in a grouping column gives the share-weighted average of the levels' b̂, and a mixture in a random-slope variable gives z at the shares (for z′b̂, and for τ under :marginal). So profile_margins(m, data, means_grid(data); random_effects = :conditional) predicts at the observed shares of every group. A nested factor's grouping columns cannot hold mixtures (the shares of its cells are not determined by those of its columns).

Standard errors

A conditional quantity depends on b̂ as well as β̂, so its standard error should include the prediction error of b̂. With re_se = :joint (the default) it does: the delta method runs over (β, b) with the joint conditional covariance of (β̂ − β, b̂ − b) given the variance parameters (Henderson's prediction-error covariance, whose β block is vcov(model)). Internally the random effects become extra coefficients of the compiled formula (full dummy columns of each grouping factor and their interactions with the slope variables), so the result's gradients and result.inference.vcov are over (β, b), and contrast, groups, scenarios and second differences of the result use the same joint covariance.

re_se = :fixed gives β-only standard errors, treating b̂ as known. These are not conservative: they can be too small or far too large. An average over the observed groups is often known much more precisely than x′β alone, since an error in β̂'s intercept is offset by the b̂ of the groups in the data. In the example the average prediction is 0.583 with standard error 0.0108 under :joint and 0.0499 under :fixed; for the AME of x they are 0.0106 and 0.0114. Use :fixed when you need a covariance other than the model's own (a user vcov), or for a rank-deficient fit; :joint requires the model's vcov and a full-rank X.

Limits

The joint path holds a dense (p + q)² covariance, so it allows at most 2000 random-effect coefficients (q). q counts every grouping factor, including those not conditioned on: the joint covariance of β̂ and the conditioned b̂ involves all of them. Above that, re_se = :joint is an error (use re_se = :fixed). re_se = :fixed above the cap uses a per-row offset instead, on which two things are not supported: contrasts of a categorical variable with a random slope, and population scenarios that set a variable with a random slope (a profile grid may set it).

random_effects = :marginal

The estimates average the inverse link over the estimated random-effects distribution, E[g⁻¹(η + z′b)] with b ~ N(0, Σ̂b). z′b is normal with variance τ² = z′Σ̂b z, so the average is one-dimensional however many grouping factors are crossed or nested, and Margins computes it by Gauss–Hermite quadrature. With random intercepts only, τ is the same for every row; with random slopes it varies by row, and Margins computes τ from each evaluated row (z's columns are added to the compiled formula with coefficient 0 and variance 0), so τ follows counterfactual rows too: scenarios, contrasts of a categorical slope variable, and profile grid rows. The effect of a random-slope variable includes the change in τ, from the formula's derivative, so any slope term (w^2 included) is exact.

re_nodes = :auto (the default) starts from 16 nodes and doubles the rule until ŷ and its first two derivatives agree with the next rule to 1e-10 at every η in [−20, 20], checked at the largest τ over the rows the request evaluates; an integer fixes the rule. metadata[:re_sd] and metadata[:re_nodes] record that τ and the rule (here 2.13 and 256 nodes). Very large τ (around 17 on the logit scale) does not converge within 1024 nodes, and the request says so.

Standard errors are delta-method on β, with the variance parameters held at their estimates.

A mode per grouping factor

With crossed raters and villages, a natural question is about the villages in the data, each rated by a new rater from the rater population:

julia> population_margins(m, d; vars = [:x], random_effects = (village = :conditional, rater = :marginal))
EffectsResult: 1 population effects (N=1440)
Random effects: predicted for village; averaged over rater (population-averaged; SD 1.99)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Variable Contrast         dy/dx   Std. Err.  [95% Conf.   Interval]
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
x        dy/dx           0.1393     0.01087       0.118      0.1606
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The villages' b̂ enter η (with joint standard errors under re_se), and the rater effects are averaged over, so τ comes from the rater terms only. Unlisted factors are set to 0. The result records metadata[:random_effects] = :hybrid and the averaged factors in metadata[:re_averaged]. A NamedTuple with one mode is that mode with re_terms set to its factors; re_terms cannot be combined with a NamedTuple. Village-level predictions follow with groups = :village.

What is held fixed

In every mode the variance parameters θ (the random-effect variances and correlations) are held at their estimates, as in R's marginaleffects. With few groups θ is poorly estimated, and the standard errors of :marginal quantities (which depend on θ through τ) and of :conditional ones (through the shrinkage of b̂) are then too small.

Recorded metadata

keyvalue
:random_effects:zero, :conditional, :marginal or :hybrid
:re_termsthe grouping factors used
:re_averaged:hybrid only: the factors averaged over
:re_se:joint or :fixed, when random effects are conditioned on
:re_sd, :re_nodeswhen random effects are averaged over: the largest τ and the rule

Validation against R

test/r_compare_marginaleffects/random_effects/ compares every mode against R, on an LMM and a logit GLMM with (1 + w | rater) + (1 | village): :zero and :conditional with re_se = :fixed against marginaleffects 1.0.0 on lme4 2.1.0 fits (re.form = NA and re.form = NULL), re_se = :joint against Henderson's equations built in R from lme4's X, Z, Λ and working weights, and :marginal against R's adaptive integrate per row. Estimates and standard errors agree to between 1e-15 and 3e-6 (the differences of the two fits).

Standard errors from lme4

lme4's default vcov for a glmer fit (use.hessian = TRUE, a finite-difference Hessian over (θ, β)) differs from MixedModels' RX-based vcov (lme4's use.hessian = FALSE), by 2.3% in the validation example. marginaleffects' default standard errors on a glmer fit therefore differ from Margins' by about that much; pass vcov = vcov(m, use.hessian = FALSE) in R to compare.

Predicted random effects of GLMMs

For a GLMM, Margins takes b̂ = Λu from the fit (m.u, what fitted(m) uses), not MixedModels.ranef, which after a fast = false fit reads a stale working state (MixedModels 4.38.1).
